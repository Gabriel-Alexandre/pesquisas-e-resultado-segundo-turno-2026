 Key Facts 
 
 The country Brazil, Latin America’s largest country, elects its president in a runoff on Sunday 25 October between President Lula and Senator Flávio Bolsonaro. 
 What happened On Friday 23 October, the polling institute Real Time Big Data released a runoff survey that puts Flávio Bolsonaro ahead by four points in valid votes. 
 The numbers Flávio has 52% of valid votes and Lula 48%, while raw totals read 49% to 45%, with 5% blank or null and 1% undecided. 
 Margin of error 2 points either way at 95% confidence, so Brazilian outlets call it a technical tie. 
 Fieldwork 2,000 voters were interviewed by telephone from Monday 19 to Thursday 22 October.

 What the Real Time Big Data Poll Found 
 The institute interviewed 2,000 voters by telephone, with human interviewers, from Monday 19 to Thursday 22 October, CNN Brasil reported. The margin of error is 2 points either way at 95% confidence, and the survey is registered with the electoral court (TSE) as BR-09670/2026.

 How This Compares With Tuesday’s Poll 
 The valid-vote split is the same as in the institute’s survey of Tuesday 20 October, O Povo reported. In raw totals, Flávio rose from 48% to 49% while Lula stayed at 45%. 
 Our report on the Tuesday Real Time Big Data poll shows that survey covered 15 to 19 October. Brazilian outlets describe the new result as a technical tie, given its 2-point margin of error. 
 How Valid Votes and Technical Ties Work 
 Brazilian election results are reported as valid votes, which leave out blank and spoiled ballots. The raw total counts every voter interviewed, so it shows how many are undecided or plan to vote for neither candidate. 
 In polling, a technical tie is the label for a result that falls within the margin of error. Brazilian outlets use it for this poll, which means it cannot say for certain which candidate is ahead.

 What Is Not Known 
 A telephone poll reaches only voters who answer, and it cannot capture any change of mind in the final two days. The survey does not show which way the 1% of undecided voters will go. 
 It is not known how many voters will turn out on 25 October. Any runoff poll measures opinion on the days of the interviews and does not forecast the result.
