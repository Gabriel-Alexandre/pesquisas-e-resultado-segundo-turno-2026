 Brazil Poll Gives Flávio 52% and Lula 48% 
 BRAZIL · ELECTIONS 
 Published Tuesday, 20 October, 8:13 a.m. ET · Source: Real Time Big Data poll, registration BR-02952/2026 
 
 Key Facts 
 
 The country Brazil, Latin America’s largest country, elects its president in a runoff on Sunday 25 October between President Lula and Senator Flávio Bolsonaro. 
 What happened On Tuesday 20 October, the polling institute Real Time Big Data released a runoff survey that puts Flávio Bolsonaro ahead by four points. 
 The numbers Flávio has 52% of valid votes and Lula 48%, while raw totals read 48% to 45%, with 5% blank or null and 2% giving no answer. 
 Margin of error 2 points either way at a 95% confidence level, so the two candidates’ ranges only just touch at 50%. 
 Fieldwork 2,000 voters were interviewed from 15 to 19 October, after the first round on 4 October. 
 Power Index Brazil ranks 1st of 14 in The Rio Times Latin America Power Index , with 75.0 points (October 2026 edition).

 Flávio Bolsonaro, senator for Rio de Janeiro and the son of former president Jair Bolsonaro, represents the conservative Liberal Party (PL). Lula, Brazil’s president, leads the left-wing Workers’ Party (PT), and the latest Brazil election polls keep the race close. 
 
 
 What the Real Time Big Data Poll Found 
 The institute interviewed 2,000 voters from 15 to 19 October by telephone and online messages, InfoMoney reported. The margin of error is 2 points either way at a 95% confidence level, and the survey is registered with the electoral court (TSE) as BR-02952/2026.

 How Valid Votes and Margins Work 
 Brazilian election results are counted in valid votes, which leave out blank and spoiled ballots. Pollsters publish that figure to match what the TSE will announce, while the raw total counts everyone interviewed. 
 A four-point gap with a 2-point margin of error means the two candidates’ ranges meet at 50%, which Bloomberg Linea Brasil described as a technical tie at the limit. The result is a snapshot of opinion on the days of the interviews, not a forecast. 
 Where the Race Stands Five Days Out 
 Flávio Bolsonaro finished first and Lula second in the first round on 4 October, with 47.03% and 45.16% of valid votes, Bloomberg Linea Brasil noted. That gap of about 2.2 million votes is the starting point for the runoff. 
 Exame headlined the survey as coming after the presidential debate hosted by broadcaster Band. Our Brazil runoff date guide explains how to follow the count on election night. 
 What It Means for US Readers 
 If you hold Brazilian stocks, bonds or US-listed shares of Brazilian companies, a race this close can mean price swings until the result is known. A four-point lead inside a 2-point margin does not make either candidate a sure winner. 
 The winner will set Brazil’s course on trade, tariffs and relations with Washington for the next four years. Our poll tracker lists every survey so you can compare the institutes. 
 What Prediction Markets Say 
 On Polymarket, an online prediction market, bettors give Flávio Bolsonaro a 76.6% chance of winning the runoff and Lula 22.5% (20 October, 8:10 a.m. ET). The “Brazil Presidential Election” market has drawn about US$188.8 million in total bets. 
 A separate Polymarket market on the margin of victory gives about 41.5% to a Flávio win by less than four points and 32.5% to a win by four to eight points. See our Brazil election odds page for live prices.

 What Is Not Known 
 The reports we reviewed do not name who commissioned the survey. It is also unclear whether the 5% of blank and null voters and the 2% with no answer will turn out on 25 October.
