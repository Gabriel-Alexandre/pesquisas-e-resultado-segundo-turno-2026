{{Politics of Peru}}

[[General elections]] were held in [[Peru]] from 12 to 13 April 2026 to elect the president, vice presidents, and the [[Congress of the Republic of Peru]]. As no presidential candidate achieved a majority of votes in the first round, a runoff election was held on 7 June.<ref name=":7" /><ref>{{Cite web |date=27 March 2025 |title=Deeply unpopular Peruvian president calls for general elections in April 2026 |url=https://apnews.com/article/peru-elections-declared-2026-president-boluarte-dabea7cd70b6288542317b080b99c8aa |access-date=27 March 2025 |website=AP NEWS |language=en |archive-date=27 March 2025 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250327164022/https://apnews.com/article/peru-elections-declared-2026-president-boluarte-dabea7cd70b6288542317b080b99c8aa |url-status=live }}</ref> In the second round, the leader of the [[Popular Force]], [[Keiko Fujimori]], defeated [[Together for Peru]] nominee [[Roberto Sánchez (Peruvian politician)|Roberto Sánchez]]. The congressional elections determined the composition of the Congress of Peru, which returned to being a [[bicameral]] legislature with a Senate (the first since the [[1990 Peruvian general election|1990 election]]) and a Chamber of Deputies.

A return to a [[bicameral]] legislative system was also established by Congress, overruling the [[2018 Peruvian constitutional referendum|2018 constitutional referendum]] where voters voted to maintain a [[unicameral]] system,<ref name=":28" /> which includes 130 seats for deputies and 60 seats for senators. Senators represent the [[upper house]] and hold more institutional control; they review bills presented by deputies and are responsible for electing the directors of the [[Central Reserve Bank of Peru]], the members of the Constitutional Court, the Comptroller General, [[Ombudsman of Peru]] and other institutional leaders. Upper house senators also hold the power to approve certain functions of the executive, such as foreign travel, and the ability to remove the president.<ref name=":3">{{Cite web |last=Espinoza |first=Sergio |date=4 March 2026 |title=Elecciones 2026: ¿cuál es la diferencia entre senadores y diputados? |url=https://rpp.pe/politica/elecciones/elecciones-2026-cual-es-la-diferencia-entre-senadores-y-diputados-noticia-1678609 |access-date=5 March 2026 |website=[[RPP (Peru)|RPP]] |language=es |archive-date=4 March 2026 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260304223913/https://rpp.pe/politica/elecciones/elecciones-2026-cual-es-la-diferencia-entre-senadores-y-diputados-noticia-1678609 |url-status=live }}</ref> The president also cannot dissolve the upper house of Congress, granting that body a large amount of power.<ref name=":13">{{Cite web |date=13 April 2026 |title=Peru election drags into second day after ballot delivery fiasco |url=https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/c4gd0l2x8j2o |access-date=14 April 2026 |website=[[BBC]] |language=en-GB |archive-date=14 April 2026 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260414011948/https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/c4gd0l2x8j2o |url-status=live }}</ref>

==Electoral system==

=== President ===
The [[President of Peru|president]] is elected using the [[two-round system]].<ref name="reuters">{{cite web|date=7 May 2021|title=Peru election race tightens as Fujimori gains, poll shows|url=https://www.reuters.com/world/americas/peru-election-race-tightens-fujimori-gains-poll-shows-2021-05-07/|url-status=live|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210607090008/https://www.reuters.com/world/americas/peru-election-race-tightens-fujimori-gains-poll-shows-2021-05-07/|archive-date=7 June 2021|access-date=7 June 2021|work=Reuters}}</ref> The first round of voting allows eligible voters to vote for any viable presidential candidate.<ref name="reuters" /> The top two candidates who receive a plurality of the vote proceed to the run-off election.<ref name="reuters" /> The winner of the run-off election and the presidential election is the candidate who receives a plurality of the popular vote.<ref name="reuters" /><ref name="nytapril2021">{{cite web|date=12 April 2021|title=In Peru's Presidential Election, the Most Popular Choice Is No One|url=https://www.nytimes.com/2021/04/11/world/americas/peru-election.html|url-status=live|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210607074429/https://www.nytimes.com/2021/04/11/world/americas/peru-election.html|archive-date=7 June 2021|work=The New York Times|access-date=7 June 2021}}</ref> However, if in the first round the candidate who is in the first place already gets more than 50% of the popular vote, that candidate will automatically win the election and a run-off election will no longer be needed.<ref name="nytapril2021" />

=== Congress and Andean Parliament ===
The 130 members of the Chamber of Deputies are elected in 27 multi-member constituencies using [[open list]] [[proportional representation]].<ref name=":0">{{Cite journal|last1=Alemán|first1=Eduardo|last2=Ponce|first2=Aldo F.|last3=Sagarzazu|first3=Iñaki|year=2011|title=Legislative Parties in Volatile, Nonprogrammatic Party Systems: The Peruvian Case in method]].<ref name=":1">[http://www.electionguide.org/countries/id/170/ Peru] {{Webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210422043153/https://www.electionguide.org/countries/id/170/|date=22 April ==== Threshold ====
To gain seats in each chamber, parties must both reach a 5% electoral threshold of valid votes for the chamber at the national level and also qualify for at least 5% of the number of seats of the respective chamber (7 for the chamber of deputies and 3 for the senate). For the senate, the combined vote total for the district and national list senators is used when checking whether a party has reached the threshold.<ref name="ellectoralsystem">{{cite news |last1=Martinez |first1=Pedro Luis Ramos |title=Elecciones 2026: JNE precisa cómo se aplicará la valla electoral para el Senado y la Cámara de Diputados |url=https://rpp.pe/politica/elecciones/elecciones-2026-jne-precisa-como-se-aplicara-la-valla-electoral-para-el-senado-y-la-camara-de-diputados-noticia-1680167 |access-date=15 April 2026 |date=15 March 2026 |language=Spanish |archive-date=16 March 2026 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260316065722/https://rpp.pe/politica/elecciones/elecciones-2026-jne-precisa-como-se-aplicara-la-valla-electoral-para-el-senado-y-la-camara-de-diputados-noticia-1680167 |url-status=live }}</ref><ref>{{cite news |last1=Talledo |first1=Valeria Mendoza |title=¿Qué pasará con los partidos que no superen la valla electoral tras los comicios de este domingo 12? |url=https://www.infobae.com/peru/2026/04/07/que-pasara-con-los-partidos-que-no-superen-la-valla-electoral-tras-los-comicios-de-este-domingo-12/ |access-date=15 April 2026 |date=7 April 2026 |language=Spanish |archive-date=10 April 2026 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260410222708/https://www.infobae.com/peru/2026/04/07/que-pasara-con-los-partidos-que-no-superen-la-valla-electoral-tras-los-comicios-de-este-domingo-12/ |url-status=live }}</ref>

== Conduct ==

=== First round ===
Days before the election, a survey showed that 80% of respondents said they did not understand the mechanisms of voting, especially with such a large ballot with multiple columns.<ref>{{Cite magazine|last=Alarcón|first=Daniel|date=2026-06-04|title=Peru's Politics Are a Disaster, but Does It Matter?|url=https://www.newyorker.com/news/the-lede/perus-politics-are-a-disaster-but-does-it-matter|access-date=2026-06-22|magazine=[[The New Yorker]]|language=en-US|issn=0028-792X|archive-date=22 June 2026|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260622214425/https://www.newyorker.com/news/the-lede/perus-politics-are-a-disaster-but-does-it-matter|url-status=live}}</ref>

[[Election monitoring]] was performed by the Electoral Observation Mission of the [[European Union]], which comprised 150 members, and 90 officials of the [[Organization of American States]] (OAS).<ref>{{Cite web |date=7 April 2026 |title=Peru's 2026 Elections: EU and OAS deploy electoral observers nationwide |url=https://andina.pe/ingles/noticia-perus-2026-elections-eu-and-oas-deploy-electoral-observers-nationwide-1070171.aspx |access-date=14 April 2026 |website=[[Andina (news agency)|Andina]] |language=es |archive-date=27 April 2026 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260427023955/https://andina.pe/ingles/noticia-perus-2026-elections-eu-and-oas-deploy-electoral-observers-nationwide-1070171.aspx |url-status=live }}</ref> Other monitoring included individuals from the [[Association of World Election Bodies]], the [[Carter Center]], the Center for Electoral Advisory and Promotion and the Inter-American Union of Electoral Organizations, alongside local monitoring groups. About 10,550 locations hosted 92,720 voting stations nationally. Internationally, 4,000 voting stations were established, though voting in Israel, Jordan, Kuwait, Lebanon, Qatar, and Saudi Arabia was cancelled due to the [[2026 Iran war]].<ref name=":14">{{Cite web |date=13 January 2026 |title=Peru's FA Min: Six international organizations to deploy electoral observation missions |url=https://andina.pe/ingles/noticia-perus-fa-min-six-international-organizations-to-deploy-electoral-observation-missions-1059056.aspx |access-date=14 April 2026 |website=[[Andina (news agency)|Andina]] |language=es |archive-date=19 April 2026 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260419045944/https://andina.pe/ingles/noticia-perus-fa-min-six-international-organizations-to-deploy-electoral-observation-missions-1059056.aspx |url-status=live }}</ref><ref name=":02" />

On election day, ballots and materials for dozens of polling stations were delivered late or not delivered at all, resulting in over 50,000 people being unable to vote. Voting issues in [[Orlando, Florida]], and [[Paterson, New Jersey]], were also reported. These complications resulted in an extension in the voting deadline for the affected voters.<ref name=":13" /> Voters in Chile, where about 113,000 Peruvians reside, expressed frustrations as well when shuttles promised to bring people to polling stations were not used and when the opening of voting at {{Interlanguage link|Espacio Riesco|es|Espacio Riesco}} was delayed by five hours, starting at 12:00 noon ([[UTC−05:00]]) instead of the planned 7:00.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Nomberto |first=Isabel |date=12 April 2026 |title=Peruanos en Chile denuncian desorden en Elecciones 2026: "Hubiera preferido pagar la multa" |url=https://larepublica.pe/sociedad/2026/04/12/peruanos-en-chile-denuncian-desorden-en-elecciones-2026-hubiera-preferido-pagar-la-multa-785256 |access-date=14 April 2026 |website=[[La República]] |language=es |archive-date=14 April 2026 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260414011013/https://larepublica.pe/sociedad/2026/04/12/peruanos-en-chile-denuncian-desorden-en-elecciones-2026-hubiera-preferido-pagar-la-multa-785256 |url-status=live }}</ref> Reports by the [[National Office of Electoral Processes]] (ONPE) showed that this delay had an insignificant effect on voter turnout.<ref name=":21"/>

[[Keiko Fujimori]] was quickly determined to be the candidate most likely heading to the second round of presidential elections. As the tabulation of voting data occurred, left-wing candidate [[Roberto Sánchez (Peruvian politician)|Roberto Sánchez]] saw his position move from 6th place to 3rd place by 15 April. While a group of [[European Union]] electoral observers and governmental authorities reported no voting irregularities, right-wing protestors gathered at the headquarters of the [[National Jury of Elections]] (JNE), participating in [[sit-in]] demonstrations.<ref name=":17" /> On 24 April 2026, the JNE ruled 3–2 against annulling the first round of elections and said that the second round would occur on its already scheduled date of 7 June 2026.<ref name=":21" />

=== Second round ===
A report reviewed by [[OjoPúblico]] showed that the scanning of ballots in the first round reportedly failed in 17 countries, and Foreign Minister [[Carlos Pareja Ríos]], a former ambassador to the United States, demanded that the Electoral Counting System (SCE) be suspended for the second round at 108 consulate offices in 59 countries, which represented about 1.1 million eligible voters.<ref name=":24">{{Cite web |last=Quispe |first=Diego |date=2026-06-21 |title=Digitalización de actas electorales del extranjero falló en al menos 17 países durante la primera vuelta |url=https://ojo-publico.com/politica/digitalizacion-actas-fallo-mitad-consulados-primera-vuelta |access-date=2026-06-23 |website=Ojo Público |language=es |archive-date=23 June 2026 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260623042439/https://ojo-publico.com/politica/digitalizacion-actas-fallo-mitad-consulados-primera-vuelta |url-status=live }}</ref> A week before the second round, the ONPE suspended the use of the SCE and decided that voting minutes would be physically transferred to Peru in [[diplomatic bag]]s and reviewed.<ref name=":24" /> The decision raised some concern from Víctor Rico Frontaura, head of the OAS monitoring group, though he said no substantial irregularities affected the second round.<ref name=":24" />

Following a complaint by journalist Karla Ramírez about irregularities in favor of Together For Peru (mainly, other ballots were marked in favor of Popular Force), the [[ONPE]] intervened, together with the national police, the [[Public Ministry of Peru|Public Ministry]], and the armed forces. It was later confirmed that 90 ballots had been previously marked, two people were arrested, and that fifty ballots had already been replaced with new ones.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Orellana |first=Daniela |date=2026-06-07 |title=Segunda Vuelta 2026: detectan cientos de cédulas marcadas previamente en locales de votación |url=https://larepublica.pe/politica/2026/06/07/segunda-vuelta-2026-dectectan-decenas-de-cedulas-previamente-marcadas-en-locales-de-votacion-durante-elecciones-presidenciales-455371 |access-date=2026-06-07 |website=larepublica.pe |language=es |archive-date=7 June 2026 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260607185336/https://larepublica.pe/politica/2026/06/07/segunda-vuelta-2026-dectectan-decenas-de-cedulas-previamente-marcadas-en-locales-de-votacion-durante-elecciones-presidenciales-455371 |url-status=live }}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |last=Bianchi |first=Jazmín |date=2026-06-07 |title=Detienen a personeros por marcar cédulas: ONPE repone el material y Fiscalía inicia diligencias |url=https://larepublica.pe/politica/2026/06/07/detienen-a-personeros-por-marcar-cedulas-onpe-repone-el-material-y-fiscalia-inicia-diligencias-hnews-243180 |access-date=2026-06-07 |website=La Republica |language=es |archive-date=7 June 2026 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260607185108/https://larepublica.pe/politica/2026/06/07/detienen-a-personeros-por-marcar-cedulas-onpe-repone-el-material-y-fiscalia-inicia-diligencias-hnews-243180 |url-status=live }}</ref>

Due to very tight results and highly likely recount, ONPE said a full count was expected to be completed by July.<ref>{{Cite web |date=2026-06-09 |title=Peru election too close to call with prospect of weeks of uncertainty |url=https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/cewq151pw78o |access-date=2026-06-09 |website=www.bbc.com |language=en-GB |archive-date=9 June 2026 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260609094852/https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/cewq151pw78o |url-status=live }}</ref>

Both Fujimori and Sánchez called for ballots to be annulled in each other's strongholds, citing alleged irregularities.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Ramos |first=Brayan |date=2026-11-06 |title=Fuerza Popular y Juntos por el Perú presentan once pedidos de nulidad de mesas de sufragio en Perú y EE.UU |url=https://ojo-publico.com/politica/elecciones-2026/fuerza-popular-y-juntos-por-el-peru-buscan-anular-votos-peru-y-eeuu |access-date=2026-06-23 |website=[[Ojo Público]] |language=es}}</ref> While Sánchez received the majority of domestic votes, Fujimori's later lead was attributed to foreign votes from expatriates located mainly in Argentina, Spain and the United States.<ref name=":25">{{Cite web |last=Ros |first=Carla Samon |date=2026-06-09 |title=Peru's diaspora emerges as tie-breaker in razor-edge presidential race |url=https://www.japantimes.co.jp/news/2026/06/09/world/politics/peru-diaspora-tie-breaker-election/ |access-date=2026-06-23 |website=The Japan Times |language=en |archive-date=10 June 2026 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260610040831/https://www.japantimes.co.jp/news/2026/06/09/world/politics/peru-diaspora-tie-breaker-election/ |url-status=live }}</ref> Following the cancellation of the SCE, the arrival commercial flights of consular staff holding foreign votes began on 10 June.<ref name=":24" /> Sánchez has not conceded and has alleged fraud, especially after the SCE suspension with international voting, saying he has instead won and would defend his "popular victory", drawing comparison to Fujimori's own initial rejection of the [[2021 Peruvian general election|2021 election]].<ref name=":24" /><ref name="AQ" /><ref name="WSJ Call" />
