In the first round, de la Espriella led with 43.7% of the vote, followed by Cepeda with 40.9%. As none of the thirteen candidates obtained at least 50% of the vote in the first round, a run-off was held. The run-off was narrowly won by de la Espriella with 49.66% of the vote against 48.70% obtained by Cepeda. With 12.9 million votes, de la Espriella became the most voted presidential candidate in Colombian history. Cepeda conceded the election on 24 June 2026, and hours later, Petro – while alleging fraud and foreign interference – also recognized the results, announcing that his administration would begin the formal handover process.<ref name=":4">{{Cite web |date=2026-06-24|title=Iván Cepeda concedes election defeat, Petro begins transition to De la Espriella government |work=The City Paper Bogotá|url=https://thecitypaperbogota.com/news/ivan-cepeda-concedes-election-defeat-petro-begins-transition-to-de-la-espriella-government/|access-date=2026-06-26|language=en-US}}</ref><ref name=":2">{{Cite web |last=Pannell |first=Alfie |title=Ivan Cepeda concedes defeat in Colombia election, sealing right-wing win |url=https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/6/24/ivan-cepeda-concedes-defeat-in-colombia-election-sealing-right-wing-win |access-date=2026-06-25 |website=Al Jazeera |language=en |quote=Colombian presidential candidate Ivan Cepeda officially conceded defeat to hard-right populist Abelardo de la Espriella this morning following a tight run-off race. == Background ==
In the previous presidential election in 2022, as none of the presidential nominees obtained at least 50% of the votes in the first round, a [[Two-round system|run-off]] was held between the top two candidates, [[Gustavo Petro]] and [[Rodolfo Hernández Suárez]].<ref>{{cite news |date=29 May 2022 |title=Colombia's presidential race heads to runoff |url=https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2022/5/29/colombias-presidential-race-heads-to-runoff |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220529231802/https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2022/5/29/colombias-presidential-race-heads-to-runoff |archive-date=29 May 2022 |access-date=29 May 2022 |work=Al Jazeera}}</ref> Petro won the run-off, becoming the first [[left-wing]] candidate to be elected [[president of Colombia]] since the country's independence in 1810.<ref>{{cite news |date=19 June 2022 |title=Leftist Gustavo Petro wins Colombian presidency |url=https://www.ft.com/content/33d30a93-0f48-41ee-9847-4503c79ca7d9 |url-access=subscription |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220620074307/https://www.ft.com/content/33d30a93-0f48-41ee-9847-4503c79ca7d9 |archive-date=20 June 2022 |access-date=19 June 2022 |work=Financial Times}}</ref><ref name="The Dialogue 2022">{{cite web |last1=Carlsen |first1=Laura |last2=Dickinson |first2=Elizabeth |last3=Dimitroff |first3=Sashe |last4=Guzmán |first4=Sergio |last5=Molina |first5=Marco |last6=Shifter |first6=Michael |last7=Velez de Berliner |first7=Maria |date=21 June 2022 |title=What Will Petro's Presidency Mean for Colombia? === Historic Pact ===
{{main|2026 Historic Pact presidential primaries}}
Cepeda announced his candidacy in July 2025 and was considered one of the initial favorites for the presidential nomination of the [[Historic Pact]] in the polls. Cepeda initially competed against six other pre-candidates, four of whom later withdrew, leaving him to face Minister [[Carolina Corcho]] and former mayor of Medellín, [[Daniel Quintero (Colombian politician)|Daniel Quintero]], in the primary election.

Following his victory in the primary election in October 2025, Cepeda officially became the presidential candidate of the Historic Pact. [[Carolina Corcho]], who received the second-highest number of votes, became the lead candidate for the Senate in the [[2026 Colombian parliamentary election|2026 parliamentary election]]s in March.{{citation needed|date=June 2026}}

After obtaining an overwhelming majority during the parliamentary election on March 8, Cepeda would announce [[Aida Quilcué]] as the candidate for [[vice president]] on Monday, March 9.

=== Democratic Centre ===
{{main|2026 Democratic Centre presidential primaries}}
Paloma Valencia became the official candidate of the [[Democratic Centre (Colombia)|Democratic Center]] after winning by a wide margin among the delegates, defeating her opponent, Senator [[María Fernanda Cabal]]. Valencia also competed in the national primary election on March 8 during the [[2026 Colombian parliamentary election|2026 parliamentary election]], where she obtained a significant majority with 3,236,286 votes, surpassing her eight fellow candidates.

Valencia announced her vice-presidential candidate on Thursday, March 12, revealing [[Juan Daniel Oviedo]], who obtained 1,255,510 votes, the second highest vote count during the national primary election.

==== 2026 ====
The CNE suspended AtlasIntel's polls (partnered with Semana) on May 19, 2026, over concerns that invalid methodologies led to biased election reporting. Specific irregularities included digital exclusion bias, model-dependent estimation, inconsistent data, quality control failures, and candidate popularity relative inflation. The ban was lifted on May 21 for procedural reasons (lack of quorum), but the technical probe remains ongoing.<ref>{{Cite web |date=2026-05-29 |title=CNE suspende encuestas de AtlasIntel por presuntas irregularidades en mediciones presidenciales de 2026 |url=https://www.agenciapi.co/noticia/elecciones-2026/cne-suspende-encuestas-de-atlasintel-por-presuntas-irregularidades-en-mediciones-presidenciales-de-2026 |access-date=2026-05-30 |website=Agenciapi.co |language=es |archive-date=20 May 2026 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260520071656/https://www.agenciapi.co/noticia/elecciones-2026/cne-suspende-encuestas-de-atlasintel-por-presuntas-irregularidades-en-mediciones-presidenciales-de-2026 |url-status=live }}</ref>

! rowspan="3" scope="col" |Blank {{efn|Under Article 258 of the Colombian Constitution, as regulated by Law 1475 of 2011, the blank vote (voto en blanco) is considered a valid vote. It is a decisive factor in the first round, as a candidate must secure an absolute majority (50% plus one) of all valid votes to win outright. If the blank vote exceeds 50% of the total valid votes cast, the election must be repeated with an entirely new slate of candidates. Historically, however, the blank vote rarely exceeds 1.75% in the first round. While polls consistently include these figures, excluding blank, null, and undecided responses often provides a clearer reflection of candidate standing; historical data suggests that many self-identified "blank" voters are, in practice, undecided voters who eventually select a candidate.}}

¹ Researchs' fieldwork:
The 12 electoral polls included in the table below—published between January and early April 2026—utilized fieldwork and data collection concluded prior to the March 20 final candidate registration deadline. 
While these surveys do not perfectly match the finalized field of 14 candidates, those excluded were minor candidates with support levels averaging less than 1% (typically between 0.1% and 0.5%).
Additionally, this data reflects the following electoral dynamics:

² Candidate Withdrawal: 
Clara López officially withdrew her candidacy after being confirmed on the final ballot to support Iván Cepeda. 
While her name remains on the legal ballot, her polling figures reflect her status prior to this withdrawal.

³ Blank Votes: 
While the "Blank Vote" (voto en blanco) is a valid legal option in Colombia, it has historically averaged only 1.5%–1.7% in first-round elections. Data suggests that many respondents who initially select "blank" or "null" are effectively undecided and often choose a candidate by election day.

⁴ Adjusted Projections: 
In several polls, calculating percentages by excluding blank, null, and undecided responses provides a clearer reflection of the leading candidates' potential to secure an absolute majority or qualify for a second round.}}

==Results==
Results from the first round indicated that [[National Salvation Movement|National Salvation]] candidate [[Abelardo de la Espriella]] led in first place, a contrast to opinion polls which suggested a lead for the [[Historic Pact]] candidate [[Iván Cepeda]]. Espriella won 43.7% of the popular vote, while Cepeda followed with 40.9% of the vote. [[Democratic Centre (Colombia)|Democratic Centre]] candidate [[Paloma Valencia]] took third place with 6.9% of the popular vote, also underperforming opinion polls that suggested higher vote percentages for Valencia. [[Sergio Fajardo]] and other candidates received 6.7% of the vote, while blank votes represented 1.73% of the vote.

The second round saw a high turnout of 63.6%, with Espriella winning with a percentage of 49.66% over Cepeda's percentage of 48.7%, a gap of roughly 250,000 votes.<ref>{{Cite news |last=Bocanegra |first=Nelson |last2=Acosta |first2=Luis Jaime |last3=Villegas |first3=Alexander |date=21 June 2026 |title=Colombian right-wing candidate De La Espriella wins tight presidential race |url=https://www.reuters.com/world/americas/colombia-votes-runoff-pitting-leftist-reformer-against-law-and-order-newcomer-2026-06-21/ |work=Reuters}}</ref> Espriella's support in the second round primarily dominated in the diaspora and in the inner departments of Colombia, while Cepeda's support was concentrated in Bogotá and the outer departments. Espriella was confirmed as the winner of the election by the [[National Electoral Council (Colombia)| National Electoral Council]] on 24 June 2026.<ref>{{Cite news |last=Garcia Cano |first=Regina |last2=Suarez |first2=Astrid |date=25 June 2026 |title=Trump-endorsed de la Espriella declared winner of Colombia’s presidential runoff election |url=https://apnews.com/article/colombia-election-ivan-cepeda-concedes-de-la-espriella-e0a39ed59a9d432d318e11c1e0735f4e |work=AP News}}</ref>

== Aftermath and reactions ==
=== First round ===
[[Flávio Bolsonaro]], a Brazilian senator and a son of [[Jair Bolsonaro]], held a call with Espriella to endorse him.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://x.com/ABDELAESPRIELLA/status/2059456888375243033|title=Abelardo De La Espriella (@ABDELAESPRIELLA) on X}}</ref> The day after the first-round election results were completed, U.S. President [[Donald Trump]] issued a long statement on [[Truth Social]] congratulating and endorsing Espriella, saying that "Espriella is a man who will fight, work, and care for his country, just like me."<ref>{{Cite web |title=Donald Trump Endorsed Abelardo de la Espriella's Presidential Candidacy in Colombia |url=https://colombiaone.com/2026/06/02/donald-trump-endorsed-abelardo-de-la-espriellas-presidential-candidacy-in-colombia/ |date=2 June 2026}}</ref> On 29 May 2026, Ecuadorean president [[Daniel Noboa]] met with Abelardo de la Espriella, and said he was committed to "jointly fight narcoterrorism", and would eliminate a security tax on 1 June. In response, the Colombian government led by Petro called the measure "deliberate interference" in the election.<ref>{{Cite web |url=https://apnews.com/article/colombia-ecuador-tariffs-election-security-tax-8cd04c6e17686dc9f833747461bb1d7b |title=Colombia accuses Ecuador of "deliberate interference" in general elections |work=AP News |date=31 May 2026 |access-date=1 June 2026 |archive-date=1 June 2026 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260601054850/https://apnews.com/article/colombia-ecuador-tariffs-election-security-tax-8cd04c6e17686dc9f833747461bb1d7b |url-status=live }}</ref>

=== Second round ===
{{external media| float = right| video1 = [https://www.youtube.com/live/4x91uF0z4uU?si=ikl9R29gnE5hgLdm Cepeda addresses the Nation after the full second round results were released] }}
Preliminary results showed de la Espriella winning the election by less than 1 percent. Petro did not accept those results,<ref name=":3">{{Cite web |date=2026-06-24 |title=Trump-endorsed de la Espriella declared winner of Colombia’s presidential runoff election |url=https://apnews.com/article/colombia-election-ivan-cepeda-concedes-de-la-espriella-e0a39ed59a9d432d318e11c1e0735f4e |access-date=2026-06-25 |website=AP News |language=en}}</ref> while Cepeda recognized them but emphasized their non-binding character,<ref name="epe_13-06-2026_vpl">{{Cite web |last=Parada Lugo |first=Valentina |date=21 June 2026 |title=Iván Cepeda reconoce el preconteo, pero pide esperar el escrutinio: “No es un dato oficial ni vinculante” |url=https://elpais.com/america-colombia/elecciones-presidenciales/2026-06-22/ivan-cepeda-reconoce-el-preconteo-pero-pide-esperar-el-escrutinio-no-es-un-dato-oficial-ni-vinculante.html |access-date=13 July 2026 |website=El País Spain |place=Bogotá |language=es}}</ref> with both saying they would wait for the results of the thorough counting of votes (''escrutinio'').<ref name=":3" /><ref name="epe_13-06-2026_vpl"/> [[Al Jazeera]] ascribed Petro's distrust in preliminary results to the [[2022 Colombian parliamentary election|2022 parliamentary election]], in which then-coalition of parties [[Historic Pact for Colombia|Historic Pact]] recovered about 500 thousand votes in the result of the ''escrutinio'' compared to preliminary results. Al Jazeera also noted that the [[National Civil Registry (Colombia)|National Civil Registry]] recorded high accuracy in the preliminary results of the [[2026 Colombian parliamentary election|2026 parliamentary election]] and in the first round of the 2026 presidential election.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Pannell |first=Alfie |title=Ivan Cepeda concedes defeat in Colombia election, sealing right-wing win |url=https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/6/24/ivan-cepeda-concedes-defeat-in-colombia-election-sealing-right-wing-win |access-date=2026-06-25 |website=Al Jazeera |language=en}}</ref> Authorities finished the recount which reaffirmed the results before declaring de la Espriella’s victory on 25 June 2026.<ref name=":3" /> Cepeda thereafter conceded the elections reaffirming he would be constructive opposition to de la Espriella, saying "I have decided to accept the result of this process, which indicates that Abelardo de la Espriella is the new president of the Republic."<ref name=":2" />

Right-wing [[Medellín]] mayor and third place candidate in the [[2022 Colombian presidential election|2022 presidential election]], [[Federico Gutiérrez]] congratulated Espriella and his running mate saying that "as the mayor of Medellin, I am ready to work with Espriella and his running mate into bringing Colombia forward."<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.youtube.com/shorts/Mf8DFx-V3YE|title=Felicito al Presidente electo Abelardo De La Espriella y Vicepresidente electo José Manuel Restrepo|via=Youtube}}</ref>{{reliable source?|date=June 2026}} Democratic Centre politician and Petro's Presidential predecessor [[Iván Duque]] congratulated Espriella and his running mate wishing them success for their administration while also thanking the [[National Civil Registry (Colombia)|National Registry]] for 'free and fair and transparent elections'.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.instagram.com/p/DZ3WCTVgX1P/|title=Instagram|website=Instagram}}</ref>{{reliable source?|date=June 2026}} [[Democratic Centre (Colombia)|Democratic Center]] leader and former president [[Álvaro Uribe]] described Espriella and his running mate as "doctors", and also expressed confidence that Espriella will establish a government of "democratic recovery" that "will benefit all Colombians", while also describing Cepeda's campaign as "illegal, with the support of Gustavo Petro". Uribe also issued claims of [[vote buying]], and "the imposition of [[Narcoterrorism|narcoterrorist groups]] that forced many communities to vote for Cepeda". Uribe also stated that "we cannot allow any more dirty tricks from the Petroist-[[Chavismo|Chavistas]]".<ref>https://www.instagram.com/p/DZ3XoSCuJi5/</ref>{{reliable source?|date=June 2026}}

de la Espriella was congratulated by U.S. President [[Donald Trump]] and Secretary of State [[Marco Rubio]], as well as by Latin American presidents such as Argentina's [[Javier Milei]], Ecuador's [[Daniel Noboa]], Chile's [[José Antonio Kast]], Bolivia's [[Rodrigo Paz]], the Dominican Republic's [[Luis Abinader]], Paraguay's [[Santiago Peña]],<ref>{{Cite web |date=2026-06-22 |title=Presidentes de Latinoamérica celebran la victoria de Abelardo De La Espriella en la segunda vuelta presidencial |url=https://www.semana.com/mundo/articulo/presidentes-de-latinoamerica-celebran-la-victoria-de-abelardo-de-la-espriella-en-la-segunda-vuelta-presidencial/202645/ |access-date=2026-06-22 |website=Semana |language=spanish |archive-date=22 June 2026 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260622101843/https://www.semana.com/mundo/articulo/presidentes-de-latinoamerica-celebran-la-victoria-de-abelardo-de-la-espriella-en-la-segunda-vuelta-presidencial/202645/ |url-status=live }}</ref> and Israeli foreign minister [[Gideon Sa'ar]].<ref>https://x.com/gidonsaar/status/2069797068961411448</ref> [[Nate Morris]], President Trump's nominee to be ambassador to Colombia, commented that he looked forward to working with de la Espriella to advance Trump’s agenda in Colombia.<ref>{{Cite news |title=Nate Morris (@NateMorris) on X |url=https://x.com/NateMorris/status/2068868484449050757 |access-date=2026-06-22 |work=X (formerly Twitter) |language=en}}</ref> The EU congratulated De la Espriella on his victory in Colombia, highlighted the peaceful elections with record participation, and offered to strengthen bilateral cooperation.<ref>{{Cite web |date=2026-06-25 |title=The EU celebrates De la Espriella's victory in Colombia |url=https://www.democrata.es/en/international/the-eu-congratulates-de-la-espriella-and-highlights-the-peaceful-nature-of-the-elections-in-colombia/ |access-date=2026-06-25 |website=Demócrata |language=en}}</ref>

==== Allegations of voter fraud ====
Petro claimed that the [[IP address]]es of several servers used in the election had been changed, and that this was proof that the result had been compromised, with him also claiming that "the only entity in the world with the capability to do that is the State of Israel". In a separate post, Petro claimed that software meant to prevent fraud had been provided by the U.S. and Israel. Petro then demanded a vote recount and an investigation into the supposed vulnerabilities. His allegations were dismissed by Attorney General Gregorio Eljach, saying that "there is no evidence of fraud".<ref>{{Cite web |date=2026-06-22 |title=Outgoing Colombian President Gustavo Petro claims Israel rigged elections |url=https://www.jpost.com/international/article-900089 |access-date=2026-06-24 |website=The Jerusalem Post |language=en}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |last=Lawal |first=Shola |title=Why is Israel being accused of meddling in Colombia presidential election? |url=https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/6/23/why-is-israel-being-accused-of-meddling-in-colombia-presidential-election |access-date=2026-06-24 |website=Al Jazeera |language=en}}</ref> After Cepeda conceded, Petro acknowledged the results, announcing that his administration would begin the formal transition process.<ref name=":4" />

At [[Corferias]], a [[convention center]] in Bogotá and Colombia's most important voting center, a person reported another person had been allowed to vote with a private credential rather than a [[Colombian identity card|national ID card]], which is the only document valid for voting.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Solis |first=Yuly |date=2026-06-21 |title=Mientras Petro alerta sobre tarjetones marcados, surge nueva denuncia en Corferias |url=https://www.colombia.com/elecciones/2026/denuncian-presunta-irregularidad-en-corferias-durante-la-segunda-vuelta-presidencial-588768 |access-date=2026-06-21 |website=Colombia.com |language=es |archive-date=21 June 2026 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260621234401/https://www.colombia.com/elecciones/2026/denuncian-presunta-irregularidad-en-corferias-durante-la-segunda-vuelta-presidencial-588768 |url-status=live }}</ref> Petro reported lawyers called for the thorough counting of votes were not allowed to enter Corferias.<ref>{{Cite web |date=2026-06-21 |title=Gustavo Petro denuncia irregularidades en escrutinio en Corferias y exige garantías para abogados: "Solo los jueces deciden el resultado" |url=https://www.semana.com/politica/articulo/gustavo-petro-denuncia-irregularidades-en-escrutinio-en-corferias-y-exige-garantias-para-abogados-solo-los-jueces-deciden-el-resultado/202620/ |access-date=2026-06-21 |website=Semana |language=es |archive-date=21 June 2026 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260621235413/https://www.semana.com/politica/articulo/gustavo-petro-denuncia-irregularidades-en-escrutinio-en-corferias-y-exige-garantias-para-abogados-solo-los-jueces-deciden-el-resultado/202620/ |url-status=live }}</ref>

Revista Raya reported that an internal document of the [[Ministry of Labour (Colombia)|Ministry of Labor]] showed that it had received complaints from the workers of 59 companies alleging that they were being constrained by company owners to vote for de la Espriella by proselytizing him and claiming that if Cepeda won, the companies would be expropriated and that employees would lose their jobs. Raya pointed out some of the companies listed were in the medical sector and had previously had allegations made against them for fraud.<ref>{{Cite web |date=2026-06-18 |title=Más de 50 empresas privadas fueron denunciadas por presionar a sus empleados para votar por De la Espriella |url=https://revistaraya.com/mas-de-50-empresas-privadas-fueron-denunciadas-por-presionar-a-sus-empleados-para-votar-por-de-la-espriella.html |access-date=2026-06-23 |website=Revista Raya |language=es-ES}}</ref>

In areas of the country where paramilitary and guerrilla organizations are present, concerns were raised before the elections by the [[Ombudsman's Office of Colombia|Ombudsman's Office]] for possible constraining of the electoral process. Right-wing senator [[María Fernanda Cabal]] claimed that guerrilla-led constraining occurred in several parts of the country in favor of Cepeda. Responses to Cabal's claims point out that the presence of armed groups in these areas do not necessarily imply constraints to the electoral process, and that many of the areas claimed to be affected by violent constraints have historically voted for progressive and left-wing candidates in the past.<ref>{{Cite web |date=2026-06-22 |title=Resultados en zonas de conflicto, existió posibles presiones al electorado durante la segunda |url=https://diariolalibertad.com/2026/06/22/resultados-en-zonas-de-conflicto-existio-posibles-presiones-al-electorado-durante-la-segunda/ |access-date=2026-06-23 |website=Diario La Libertad |language=es}}</ref> Claims similar to Cabal's were also made by De la Espriella's campaign. The Election Observation Mission of the European Union, in a report about its monitoring the elections, did not find meddling from armed groups that could have affected the results of the elections.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Pérez Martínez |first=Juan David |date=2026-09-03 |title=“Transparentes y organizadas”: El balance de la MOE-UE sobre las elecciones |url=https://www.lasillavacia.com/en-vivo/transparentes-y-organizadas-el-balance-de-la-moe-ue-sobre-las-elecciones/ |access-date=2026-09-10 |website=La Silla Vacía |language=es-CO}}</ref>
